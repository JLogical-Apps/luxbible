# Product

## Overview

Lux Memory is a focused Scripture memorization app intended to help Christians learn passages accurately and retain them over time. It is planned as a separate app from Lux Bible so that reading and study can remain distinct from the daily rhythm of learning and review.

Lux Memory belongs to the Lux product family:

- Lux Bible is where people read, annotate, and study Scripture.
- Lux Memory is where people intentionally learn and retain Scripture.
- Each app should remain useful on its own while offering meaningful integration with the other.

The working product name is **Lux Memory**. The brand should inherit Lux's calm, modern, serious approach without requiring both apps or making memorization feel like an additional Bible feature.

## Purpose

Many Christians want to memorize Scripture but struggle to choose passages, build a sustainable habit, know how to practice, and continue reviewing what they once learned. Existing tools can feel visually dated, overly gamified, unfocused, or dependent on paid features.

Lux Memory should make Scripture memorization approachable without trivializing it. Its primary measure of success is durable recall, not time spent in the app, points earned, or a streak preserved.

## Audience

Lux Memory is primarily for Christians who want a focused practice for remembering Scripture, including:

- People beginning Scripture memorization for the first time
- People returning after previous attempts faded
- Existing Lux Bible readers who want to memorize passages they encounter while reading or studying
- People memorizing individual verses, topical collections, chapters, or whole books
- Families and friends who may eventually want to encourage one another

The app can appeal particularly to digitally fluent younger adults through its design, but age is not a product boundary.

## Positioning

Lux Memory aims to be:

- More focused and visually calm than heavily gamified habit apps
- More approachable and modern than traditional Scripture memory tools
- More serious about long-term retention than apps centered on passive exposure or simple recognition games
- Free and useful without an account
- Closely connected to biblical context and study without becoming a second Bible app

It is not intended to be a devotional platform, social network, general Christian content app, or replacement for reading Scripture in context.

## Product Principles

### Scripture First

The passage remains the center of the experience. Progress systems, activities, reminders, and visual metaphors should support attention to Scripture rather than compete with it.

### Recall Over Engagement

Lux Memory should optimize for accurate, durable recall rather than maximizing sessions, notifications, streaks, or time in the app. Assisted activities can prepare users, but meaningful progress should depend on recalling Scripture with decreasing help across time.

### Understanding Before Repetition

Memorization should remain connected to context and meaning. Lux Memory should encourage people to understand the passage they are learning and make it easy to study the surrounding Scripture in Lux Bible.

### Sustainable Practice

Daily sessions should respect the amount of time a person intends to give. The app should protect previously learned passages, introduce new material at a sustainable pace, and help users recover without shame when review work accumulates.

### Encouragement Without Pressure

Progress should feel rewarding without turning spiritual discipline into competition or guilt. Missed days should not destroy visible progress, and social accountability should remain mutual, private, and optional.

### Private and Offline First

Core memorization should work without an account and keep user activity on the device. Any future account, synchronization, or social feature should be optional and collect only the information required for the feature the user enables.

### Free

Every memorization feature should remain free. Lux Memory should have no ads, subscriptions, or paid upgrades. Optional financial support may be explored separately without affecting access or progress.

### Editorial Transparency

Users should be able to choose their own passages. Curated topical or doctrinal collections should identify their source rather than presenting one tradition's selection as Lux's neutral editorial conclusion.

### Independently Complete, Better Together

Lux Memory should not require Lux Bible. Integrations should make passage selection, study, and navigation more convenient without making either app incomplete on its own.

## Design Direction

Lux Memory should share Lux's typography, restraint, quality, and light-inspired identity while having a warmer personality suited to practice and growth.

A quiet garden metaphor is being explored:

- Passages can be represented as growing in the user's memory.
- Progress may use language such as Planted, Sprouting, Taking Root, and Flourishing.
- Passages ready for review may Need Tending.
- Intentionally paused passages may be Dormant.

The metaphor should remain understandable, optional in emphasis, and subordinate to literal progress information. Plants should not die or visibly deteriorate because someone missed a day. Visual growth should reflect meaningful recall rather than opening the app or completing easy activities.

## Community Direction

Lux Memory should initially share Lux's existing social presence and Discord community, with product-specific content and channels as needed.

Future accountability may allow friends or family to share selected goals and completion milestones. This should begin with private, invite-only relationships and limited encouragement rather than public rankings or unrestricted messaging.

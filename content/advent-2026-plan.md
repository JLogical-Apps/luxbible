# The Coming of Christ: A 27-Day Advent Study

Across these 27 days, we’ll follow people who hoped for God to act. Some heard promises that reached beyond their own lives. Others got to meet the promised king. The final six days follow the work of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit in bringing that hope to us.

For Advent 2026, start November 29 to finish on Christmas Day. Begin with the Bible readings and take a closer look at one word from the day’s passage. Additional Reading links are there when you have more time.

## The promise before Bethlehem

### Day 1 — Eve: Looking ahead to her offspring

**Read**

[Genesis 3:1–21](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gen.3.1-Gen.3.21) · [Genesis 4:25](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gen.4.25) · [1 John 3:8](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/1John.3.8)

When God gives his ruling in the garden, he tells the serpent that Eve’s offspring will strike its head. That gives Eve’s family something to hope for after they leave the garden.

After Abel is killed, Eve calls Seth another child God has given her. We can follow the promise further than Eve could see, to Jesus, who came to undo the devil’s work.

**Go Deeper**

[“Seed” in Genesis 3:15](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gen.3.15). Hebrew *zeraʿ* (H2233). This word can mean a seed you plant or the children who come after you. Eve uses the same word again when she speaks of Seth.

### Day 2 — Abraham: A blessing for every family

**Read**

[Genesis 12:1–9](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gen.12.1-Gen.12.9) · [Galatians 3:6–16](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gal.3.6-Gal.3.16)

God calls Abram away from home with a promise big enough for every family on earth. Abram sets out before he has a child to carry that blessing forward.

Paul follows that promise all the way to Jesus. The hope that began in one family reaches people of every nation who put their trust in Christ.

**Go Deeper**

[“Seed” in Galatians 3:16](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gal.3.16). Greek *sperma* (G4690). A seed can mean someone’s offspring. Paul draws our eye to Jesus as the descendant through whom God keeps his promise to Abraham.

### Day 3 — Tamar: A future for her family

**Read**

[Genesis 38](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gen.38) · [Matthew 1:1–3](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.1.1-Matt.1.3)

Tamar’s future is uncertain after her husband dies. Judah tells her to wait for his son Shelah. When he fails to keep his word, Tamar acts so she can have a child. When he finally sees what he has done, he says she was in the right.

Tamar’s son Perez carries that family forward. Matthew names them both in the family line of Jesus, where we can see how far God carried a story that once looked like a dead end.

**Go Deeper**

[“More righteous” in Genesis 38:26](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gen.38.26). Hebrew *tsadaq* (H6663). Here the verb means to be in the right. Judah has to admit that Tamar has the better claim. His words turn the story around.

### Day 4 — Rahab: Hoping for rescue

**Read**

[Joshua 2:1–21](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Josh.2.1-Josh.2.21) · [Joshua 6:22–25](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Josh.6.22-Josh.6.25) · [Hebrews 11:31](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Heb.11.31)

Rahab has heard what God has done for Israel. As Jericho waits in fear, she trusts the Lord enough to shelter the spies and asks them to keep her family safe.

Her family is spared, and Rahab finds a place among God’s people. Matthew gives us another glimpse of where her story leads by naming her in Jesus’ family line.

**Go Deeper**

[“Kindness” in Joshua 2:12](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Josh.2.12). Hebrew *hesed* (H2617). Rahab uses the same word for the kindness she showed the spies and the kindness she asks them to show her family. Here it is loyalty you can count on when you’re in danger.

### Day 5 — Ruth: Finding refuge

**Read**

[Ruth 1:6–18](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Ruth.1.6-Ruth.1.18) · [Ruth 2:8–12](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Ruth.2.8-Ruth.2.12) · [Ruth 4:13–22](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Ruth.4.13-Ruth.4.22) · [Matthew 1:5–6](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.1.5-Matt.1.6)

Ruth leaves Moab with Naomi. She has lost her husband and has no sure way of making a living, but she chooses Naomi’s people and Naomi’s God. In Bethlehem, Boaz makes sure she can gather food safely.

Ruth is looking for a place to live and belong. The story ends with a family that will give Israel a king. Matthew carries that same family line forward to Jesus.

**Go Deeper**

[“Take refuge” in Ruth 2:12](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Ruth.2.12). Hebrew *chasah* (H2620). To take refuge is to look for shelter. Boaz pictures Ruth doing that under God’s wings, close enough to be protected.

**Read**

[2 Samuel 7:8–29](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/2Sam.7.8-2Sam.7.29)

David wants to build God a house. God gives David a promise of his own. His family will have a throne that lasts beyond his own lifetime.

When Gabriel tells Mary that her son will receive David’s throne, the promise sounds familiar. Jesus is the king whose reign will have no end.

[“House” in 2 Samuel 7:11](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/2Sam.7.11). Hebrew *bayit* (H1004). A house can be a building or a family. David has a building in mind. God uses the same word to promise him a family of kings.

### Day 7 — Isaiah: Hoping for God to be with us

**Read**

[Isaiah 7:10–16](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Isa.7.10-Isa.7.16) · [Isaiah 9:1–7](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Isa.9.1-Isa.9.7)

Judah is afraid of its enemies when Isaiah brings King Ahaz the sign of Immanuel. Isaiah also speaks of a child whose rule will bring justice and peace. Listen to how those promises sound to people facing danger.

Matthew hears Isaiah’s words again in the story of Jesus’ birth. The hope of God being with his people comes close enough for Mary to hold in her arms.

**Go Deeper**

[“Immanuel” in Isaiah 7:14](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Isa.7.14). Hebrew *Immanuel* (H6005). The name brings together the words for “with us” and “God.” In the middle of Judah’s fear, the child’s name gives people a promise they can say out loud.

### Day 8 — Micah: Waiting for a shepherd king

**Read**

[Micah 5:1–5](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Mic.5.1-Mic.5.5)

Micah gives people under threat a small place to look to. From Bethlehem will come a ruler who stands in the Lord’s strength and gives his people room to live in peace.

Micah pictures the king tending a flock. That is the hope Bethlehem holds out. God’s promised ruler will use his strength to care for his people.

**Go Deeper**

[“Shepherd” in Micah 5:4](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Mic.5.4). Hebrew *raʿah* (H7462). This verb means to tend or feed a flock. Micah gives us a king doing a shepherd’s everyday work of caring for the ones in his charge.

### Day 9 — Malachi: Making room for the Lord

**Read**

[Malachi 3:1–4](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Mal.3.1-Mal.3.4) · [Malachi 4:1–6](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Mal.4.1-Mal.4.6) · [Luke 1:16–17](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.16-Luke.1.17)

The people Malachi speaks to are looking for the Lord to come. God promises a messenger to get them ready. He also promises to clean up what has gone wrong among his people.

Gabriel says John will help turn people back to God. Advent gives us time to wait in that same way, ready to let God change what needs to change in us.

**Go Deeper**

[“Messenger” in Malachi 3:1](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Mal.3.1). Hebrew *malakh* (H4397). A messenger is someone sent with a word. Malachi points beyond the one who prepares the road to the Lord who is coming down it.

## Hearing the news

### Day 10 — Zechariah: Waiting for the dawn

**Read**

[Luke 1:18–22](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.18-Luke.1.22) · [Luke 1:57–80](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.57-Luke.1.80)

Zechariah has trouble believing the promise of a son after years of waiting. When he can finally speak again, he praises God for remembering a promise bigger than the one made to his family.

He sings of a light coming for people who sit in darkness. John will get them ready for the one who brings that light and leads them toward peace.

**Go Deeper**

[“Dawn” in Luke 1:78](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.78). Greek *anatolē* (G395). The word can describe the rising of the sun. Zechariah pictures God’s mercy arriving like the first light after a long night.

### Day 11 — Elizabeth: Believing God will keep his word

**Read**

[Luke 1:24–25](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.24-Luke.1.25) · [Luke 1:39–45](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.39-Luke.1.45)

Elizabeth has waited a long time for a child of her own. When Mary visits, the Holy Spirit fills Elizabeth, and she welcomes Mary as the mother of her Lord.

Elizabeth blesses Mary for trusting what God said before she can see it all come true. Her welcome gives Mary someone to share the hope of Jesus’ arrival with.

**Go Deeper**

[“Believed” in Luke 1:45](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.45). Greek *pisteuō* (G4100). To believe here is to trust the one who spoke. Elizabeth calls Mary blessed for trusting that the Lord will keep his word.

### Day 12 — Gabriel: Announcing the promised king

**Read**

[Luke 1:13–15](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.13-Luke.1.15) · [Luke 1:30–33](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.30-Luke.1.33)

Gabriel brings news of two children. John will have a part in getting people ready. Mary’s child will receive the throne God promised to David.

Gabriel tells Mary to look beyond the birth itself. The son she is waiting for will be king forever. The promise is much bigger than anything she can see that day.

**Go Deeper**

[“Throne” in Luke 1:32](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.32). Greek *thronos* (G2362). A throne is the seat of a ruler. Gabriel uses it to tie the child Mary is expecting to David’s family and to the kingdom God promised.

### Day 13 — Mary: Singing while she waits

**Read**

[Luke 1:46–56](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.46-Luke.1.56)

Mary is still waiting to hold her son when she sings about what God has done. She sees her own story as part of the mercy God promised to Abraham and his family.

Her hope makes room for people who are hungry, overlooked, or brought low. She can praise God while she waits because she trusts the one who made the promise.

**Go Deeper**

[“Magnifies” in Luke 1:46](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.46). Greek *megalynō* (G3170). To magnify the Lord here is to speak of his greatness. Mary’s song lets us hear how big God’s mercy looks to her.

### Day 14 — Joseph: Trusting the promise in Jesus’ name

**Read**

[Matthew 1:18–25](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.1.18-Matt.1.25) · [Matthew 2:19–23](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.2.19-Matt.2.23)

Joseph is about to change his plans when an angel tells him to take Mary as his wife and name the child Jesus. That name comes with a promise. The child will save his people from their sins.

Joseph takes Mary and the child into his care. Later, when it is time to come back from Egypt, he still has to trust God with where the family will live.

**Go Deeper**

[“Save” in Matthew 1:21](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.1.21). Greek *sōzō* (G4982). This verb means to save or rescue. The angel tells Joseph exactly what the promised child will rescue his people from.

### Day 15 — John the Baptist: Watching for the one to come

**Read**

[John 1:19–34](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/John.1.19-John.1.34)

John has been getting people ready for someone greater. When people come asking whether he is the promised one, he describes himself as a voice preparing the way.

Then he sees Jesus coming and calls him the Lamb of God. John can finally point to the one he has been telling people to look for.

**Go Deeper**

[“Lamb” in John 1:29](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/John.1.29). Greek *amnos* (G286). A lamb is a familiar picture from Israel’s sacrifices. John uses it to name the one who will take away the sin of the world.

**Additional Reading**

## Meeting the promised king

### Day 16 — The shepherds: Going to see their Savior

**Read**

[Luke 2:1–20](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.2.1-Luke.2.20)

The shepherds are keeping watch in the fields when an angel tells them their Savior has been born. They have a sign to look for. They will find a baby lying in a manger.

They hurry to Bethlehem and find what the angel told them they would. The promise they heard in the dark turns into news they can share and a reason to praise God.

**Go Deeper**

[“Christ” in Luke 2:11](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.2.11). Greek *christos* (G5547). Christ means “anointed one,” the title of God’s chosen king. The angel gives that title to the child the shepherds are about to meet.

### Day 17 — Simeon: Holding the one he waited for

**Read**

[Luke 2:25–35](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.2.25-Luke.2.35)

The Holy Spirit has told Simeon he will see the Lord’s Christ before he dies. When the Spirit leads him to the temple, he takes Jesus in his arms and sees the salvation he has been waiting for.

Simeon thanks God for light reaching the nations as well as glory for Israel. He also tells Mary that people will oppose Jesus.

**Go Deeper**

[“Consolation” in Luke 2:25](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.2.25). Greek *paraklēsis* (G3874). Here it means comfort. Simeon is waiting for God to come to Israel’s aid, like someone waiting for help they cannot bring about themselves.

### Day 18 — Anna: Sharing the hope of rescue

**Read**

[Luke 2:36–38](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.2.36-Luke.2.38) · [Isaiah 52:7–10](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Isa.52.7-Isa.52.10)

Anna has spent years praying and worshiping. When she sees Jesus at the temple, she thanks God and tells the people who have been waiting for Jerusalem to be rescued.

Isaiah gives words to that hope as he pictures a messenger announcing that God has come to rescue his people.

**Go Deeper**

[“Redemption” in Luke 2:38](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.2.38). Greek *lytrōsis* (G3085). Redemption here is release or rescue. Anna speaks to people who are waiting for God to set Jerusalem free.

### Day 19 — The chief priests and scribes: Knowing where to look

**Read**

[Matthew 2:1–6](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.2.1-Matt.2.6)

When Herod asks where the promised king will be born, the chief priests and scribes know where to look. They turn to Scripture and point him to Bethlehem.

Their answer gives us a place to watch for and a picture of the king to expect. The ruler they read about will care for Israel as a shepherd cares for a flock.

**Go Deeper**

[“Will shepherd” in Matthew 2:6](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.2.6). Greek *poimainō* (G4165). The verb means to tend a flock. The leaders’ answer tells Herod how this king will lead as well as where he will be born.

### Day 20 — Herod: Fearing the king others hoped for

**Read**

[Matthew 2:7–8](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.2.7-Matt.2.8) · [Matthew 2:13–18](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.2.13-Matt.2.18) · [Psalm 2](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Ps.2)

Herod wants to find the child for a very different reason from the visitors. A king born in Bethlehem threatens the throne Herod wants to keep. His fear brings grief to families there.

Psalm 2 gives us words for a ruler’s resistance to God’s chosen king. In Matthew, God warns Joseph and the child escapes to Egypt. The king’s story will go on even as Matthew makes room for the families’ grief.

**Go Deeper**

[“Anointed One” in Psalm 2:2](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Ps.2.2). Hebrew *mashiach* (H4899). This is the word we get “Messiah” from. In the psalm, the rulers take their stand against the king God has chosen.

### Day 21 — The Magi: Finding the king they came to see

**Read**

[Matthew 2:9–12](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.2.9-Matt.2.12) · [Psalm 72:8–11](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Ps.72.8-Ps.72.11)

The Magi travel from the east looking for the king of the Jews. When they see the star again, their joy tells us how much they have hoped to find him.

They come into the house, see Jesus with Mary, and bow down. Psalm 72 helps us see how far the hope of God’s king can reach. Even people from far away come to honor him.

**Go Deeper**

[“Bowed in worship” in Matthew 2:11](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Matt.2.11). Greek *proskyneō* (G4352). This word can mean to bow down in worship or honor. The Magi let their bodies and their gifts show how they see the child.

## The Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit

### Day 22 — The Father: Planning a place for us in his family

**Read**

[Galatians 4:1–7](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gal.4.1-Gal.4.7) · [Ephesians 1:3–14](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Eph.1.3-Eph.1.14)

For the last six days, we’ll look at how the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit bring about the hope we’ve been following. Paul says the Father sends his Son at the right time so we can become his children.

Ephesians traces that welcome back before the world began. Galatians brings it into our hearts. God sends the Spirit so we can call him Father.

**Go Deeper**

[“Adoption” in Galatians 4:5](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Gal.4.5). Greek *huiothesia* (G5206). The word means adoption as a son, with a place and an inheritance in the family. In Christ we receive the welcome Paul is talking about.

### Day 23 — The Father: Sending the Son in love

**Read**

[1 John 4:7–14](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/1John.4.7-1John.4.14) · [John 3:16–21](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/John.3.16-John.3.21)

John tells us what God’s love looks like. The Father sends his Son into a world that needs saving so we can live through him. At Christmas we see that love take the first step toward us.

The Father’s gift gives us hope when our own love falls short. We can love someone today because God has already loved us.

[“Atoning sacrifice” in 1 John 4:10](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/1John.4.10). Greek *hilasmos* (G2434). John uses this word for the sacrifice that deals with our sin. The Father’s love reaches all the way to what it costs to bring us back to him.

People had been waiting for God to come near. John takes us back before Bethlehem to show us who came. The eternal Word, who was with God and was God, became human and lived among us.

Philippians shows us what it meant for Jesus to come so close. He willingly took the place of a servant. The king people had waited for came ready to give himself for them.

[“Made his dwelling” in John 1:14](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/John.1.14). Greek *skēnoō* (G4637). The verb can mean to pitch a tent or live somewhere. John pictures the Son making his home among us, close enough for people to see his glory.

### Day 26 — The Holy Spirit: Caring for Mary as she waits

**Read**

[Luke 1:34–38](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.34-Luke.1.38) · [1 Peter 1:10–12](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/1Pet.1.10-1Pet.1.12)

Mary has a promise to hold on to before she can see it fulfilled. Gabriel tells her the Holy Spirit will come upon her, then gives her news of Elizabeth. When Mary visits, the Spirit fills Elizabeth and she blesses Mary for trusting God.

God gives Mary someone to share the waiting with. In time, Mary holds the child he promised. The Spirit who gave the prophets hope for Christ is at work as God keeps his word to her.

[“Word” in Luke 1:38](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Luke.1.38). Greek *rhēma* (G4487). Here it means what was spoken. Gabriel says God’s word will hold, and Mary entrusts what comes next to that very word.

### Day 27 — The Holy Spirit: Helping us trust Jesus today

**Read**

[John 16:7–15](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/John.16.7-John.16.15) · [Romans 8:14–17](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Rom.8.14-Rom.8.17) · [Romans 8:26–27](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Rom.8.26-Rom.8.27)

The Holy Spirit helped Elizabeth, Zechariah, and Simeon see what God was doing as they waited for Jesus. The same Spirit points us to Jesus today. He assures God’s children they belong to him and helps them pray when words fail.

Jesus died for our sins and rose again. You can come to him today with one real thing you’re facing. Ask the Holy Spirit to help you turn from sin, trust Jesus in that situation, and love the people he’s put in your life.

**Go Deeper**

[“Helps us” in Romans 8:26](https://app.luxbible.app/passage/Rom.8.26). Greek *synantilambanomai* (G4878). The word means to come to someone’s aid. Paul uses it for the Spirit’s help when we’re weak and don’t know what to pray.
